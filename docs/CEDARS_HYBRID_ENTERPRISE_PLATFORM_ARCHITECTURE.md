## 1. Four economic layers — canonical hierarchy

Every Grid belongs to one parent economic layer. Each layer and each Grid has a separately governed desired state. A Grid is effectively enabled only when both its parent layer and its own desired state are ON. Turning a parent layer OFF isolates its child Grids without erasing their individual desired states.

## 5. AI, compute, robotics and endpoints

The architecture includes AI agents and models; tools and compute; connected endpoints; Robotics + AI; Collective Memory; and the Innovation Dome™ workspace.

Robotics + AI follows **sense → plan → act → report evidence**. Robots and consequential actions require separate authorization, safety controls, human oversight and emergency stop capability.

Collective Memory follows **capture → structure → connect → reuse**. The Innovation Dome™ includes Memory Wall, Expert Wall, Innovation Graph, Future Simulator and Contribution Ledger.

## 6. AI-worker task workflow

The target workflow is AI-worker automated: discover → match → route → execute within approved permissions → verify → record → learn. Human oversight is reserved for governance, approvals, exceptions, safety and appeals; the ordinary workflow is not defined as manual work.

Current prototype facts:
- Eight AI worker catalogue entries are present.
- Autonomous AI worker execution runtime is NOT CONNECTED.
- Task entry and queue behavior in the prototype are local demonstration behavior only.
- No worker may issue assets, create monetary claims, approve its own work, alter governance policy, or execute financial or blockchain actions.

## 7. Grid verification and maintenance

Each Grid should have:
- Canonical parent layer and Grid identifier.
- Named accountable owner and backup.
- Purpose, scope, service category and dependencies.
- Desired state, effective state and separately evidenced operational status.
- Verification criteria, source evidence, evidence timestamp and reviewer.
- Provider qualifications, buyer validation, capacity and lawful-delivery checks where commercial.
- Runtime health, integration status, audit events and incident history.
- Maintenance owner, corrective actions, last review and next review date.
- Change history, approval authority and rollback/isolation plan.

The current prototype supplies search and a checklist aid. Its owner, review-date and maintenance fields are not persisted; the checklist is not a completed verification record. Backend maintenance records, reminders and evidence workflows remain future implementation work. No Grid may be marked LIVE by assertion.

## 9. CEDARS Coin™ and shared Treasury

CEDARS Coin™ is a proposed combined ecosystem model connecting ecosystem identity, verified contribution, service utility, Bitcoin-compatible technology research and a real monetary-value dimension.

Its monetary valuation belongs in the same shared CEDARS Treasury, not a separate Coin treasury. Contribution records do not automatically become money; service entitlements do not automatically become transferable tokens; Bitcoin compatibility does not imply Bitcoin backing.

The existing `GET /api/admin/treasury` endpoint is a read-only external-chain observation surface and is not a Coin valuation source. The proposed `GET /api/admin/treasury/coin-valuation` endpoint is NOT IMPLEMENTED. A read-only valuation register requires an approved source, methodology, denomination, timestamp, audit trail, independent reconciliation and backend Command Centre-only RBAC. No sample amounts may be fabricated. A valuation record is not authority to transfer funds.
